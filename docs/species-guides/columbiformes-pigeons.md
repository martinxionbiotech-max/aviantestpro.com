# Columbiformes (Pigeons & Doves)


## Overview

Pigeons represent a significant portion of SENO's testing volume, particularly racing pigeons (homing pigeons). SENO processes **600+ performance gene tests** and **500+ virus PCR tests** from pigeon samples monthly. The pigeon racing industry — particularly strong in Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, Taiwan, China, and the Middle East — drives demand for comprehensive genetic services that go beyond basic DNA sexing.

## Background

Columbiformes (pigeons and doves) encompass over 300 species worldwide. The domestic pigeon (*Columba livia domestica*) was one of the first birds domesticated by humans (~5,000+ years ago), and selective breeding has produced an extraordinary diversity of breeds — from the high-velocity Racing Homer to the flamboyant Fantail and the vocal Trumpeter.

Pigeons differ from psittacines in several important respects for genetic testing:

- **CHD sexing is straightforward:** ~25–30 bp fragment size difference means gel separation is reliable
- **Disease profile is distinct:** PiCV, PPMV-1, and PiHV are the primary concerns, not PBFD
- **Performance genetics is a major field:** Unlike parrots, pigeons have well-studied performance markers
- **Sample size is smaller:** 300–500 g body weight makes blood collection safe and easy

## Complete Testing Services for Pigeons

### DNA Sexing

- CHD-based PCR sexing using P2/P8 primer set
- > 99.5% accuracy with feather or blood samples
- Reliable from day-old squab to adult
- Sex ratio confirmation for balanced loft management

### Performance Gene Testing

| Gene | Function | Available Markers | Clinical Application |
|:-----|:---------|:-----------------:|:--------------------|
| DRD4 | Dopamine receptor — homing motivation, behaviour | 5 polymorphisms | Racing motivation / homing drive |
| LDHA | Lactate dehydrogenase — anaerobic metabolism | 3 polymorphisms | Endurance capacity |
| CK (Creatine Kinase) | Muscle energy metabolism | 2 polymorphisms | Sprint / explosive performance |
| MB (Myoglobin) | Oxygen transport in muscle | 2 polymorphisms | Stamina and recovery |

See [Performance Gene Testing](../pigeon-genetics/performance-gene-testing.md) for full details.

### DNA Fingerprinting

- Microsatellite-based individual genetic profile
- 8–12 polymorphic microsatellite markers per bird
- Applications: individual identification, theft prevention, registration verification

### Parentage Verification

- Genetic comparison of offspring with putative parents
- Confirms or excludes parent-offspring relationships
- Essential for pedigree validation and breeding record integrity

See [Parentage Verification](../pigeon-genetics/parentage-verification.md) for methodology.

### Disease Testing (11-Pathogen Panel)

| # | Pathogen | Type | Primary Concern for Racing Pigeons |
|:-:|:---------|:-----|:----------------------------------|
| 1 | Pigeon Herpes Virus (PiHV) | DNA virus | Respiratory distress, performance drop |
| 2 | Pigeon Circovirus (PiCV) | DNA virus | Immunosuppression — young bird disease |
| 3 | Pigeon Adenovirus (PiADV) | DNA virus | Hepatic necrosis, enteritis |
| 4 | Pigeon Paramyxovirus-1 (PPMV-1) | RNA virus | Neurological signs — race killer |
| 5 | Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV) | RNA virus | Respiratory / neurological disease |
| 6 | Avian Influenza Virus (AIV) | RNA virus | Respiratory, drop in performance |
| 7 | *Salmonella enterica* | Bacteria | Enteritis, septicemia |
| 8 | *Escherichia coli* (pathogenic) | Bacteria | Enteritis, colisepticemia |
| 9 | *Chlamydia psittaci* | Bacteria | Respiratory, zoonotic risk |
| 10 | *Mycoplasma* spp. | Bacteria | Respiratory, airsacculitis |
| 11 | *Candida albicans* | Fungus | Crop mycosis |

## Recommended Testing Schedule

### Pre-Breeding (January–February)

| Test | Purpose |
|:-----|:--------|
| DNA sexing for unconfirmed pairs | Ensure sex-balanced pairs for breeding |
| Parentage verification for pedigree records | Validate studbook entries before pairing |
| Performance gene testing | Identify genetic potential for selective breeding |
| Disease panel screening | Ensure breeding stock is healthy before pairing |

### Pre-Racing Season (March–April)

| Test | Purpose |
|:-----|:--------|
| Performance gene testing | Understand team genetic composition |
| Disease panel screening (11 pathogens) | Ensure birds enter season healthy |
| DNA fingerprinting | Permanent individual identification |
| AIV screening | Regulatory compliance for race events |

### During Season (May–September)

| Test | Purpose |
|:-----|:--------|
| Disease testing for performance drops | Diagnostic — identify cause of poor form |
| Post-race health screening | After exposure at one-loft races or shows |
| Single pathogen PCR (suspected infection) | Targeted testing based on symptoms |

### Post-Season (October–December)

| Test | Purpose |
|:-----|:--------|
| Comprehensive health panel | End-of-season health assessment |
| Fingerprinting for breeding stock | Confirm identification for winter pairing |
| Parentage verification | Confirm offspring from recorded pairings |

## Quantity vs. Quality: Building a Genetic Profile Database

For serious racing lofts, SENO recommends building a genetic database over multiple seasons:

| Year | Recommended Testing | Cumulative Data Value |
|:----:|:-------------------|:---------------------|
| 1 | Sexing + performance markers on all birds | Baseline genetic profile |
| 2 | Add fingerprinting + parentage verification | Pedigree validation |
| 3 | Annual disease screening + new bird profiling | Comprehensive loft health |
| 4+ | Comparative analysis of genetic markers vs. race results | Data-driven breeding decisions |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How early can I sex a racing pigeon?**
A: From day one. The CHD gene sequence is determined at fertilization — a squab's DNA carries the same sex information as an adult's. No need to wait for behavioral cues or sexual maturity (6–12 months).

**Q: Can DNA fingerprinting replace bands/rings?**
A: No — rings are the primary identification for competition. However, fingerprinting serves as a secondary "genetic ring" that uniquely identifies the bird if a ring is lost, damaged, or swapped.

**Q: How is the performance gene test useful if it doesn't predict racing winners?**
A: The test identifies genetic potential, not racing results. Used over multiple seasons and across a whole loft population, it helps breeders make data-informed pairing decisions — biasing towards combinations of favourable marker alleles that correlate with improved racing performance in population studies.

**Q: What is the most common health issue in racing pigeons detected by PCR?**
A: PiCV (Pigeon Circovirus) is the most frequently detected pathogen in routine screening panels. PiCV causes immunosuppression that predisposes birds to secondary infections with other pathogens. It is particularly common in young birds and often underdiagnosed clinically.

**Q: Do you test for Trichomonas?**
A: Trichomonas (canker) is a protozoal infection rather than a viral/bacterial pathogen, and is not included in SENO's standard 11-pathogen PCR panel. Trichomonas is typically diagnosed by microscopic examination of oral swabs by a veterinarian.
