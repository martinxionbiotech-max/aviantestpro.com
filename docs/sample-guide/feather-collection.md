# Feather Sample Collection Protocol


Feather samples are the most commonly submitted sample type to SENO's avian genetics laboratory. When collected correctly, freshly plucked feathers provide reliable DNA for sexing, genetic disease screening, and species identification. This protocol covers proper plucking technique, quality control, species-specific guidance, and packaging standards.

## Why Freshly Plucked Feathers Are Essential

DNA quality is the single most important factor for accurate testing. **Feathers must be freshly plucked** — the DNA in feather samples comes exclusively from the follicle cells at the base of the feather shaft. Shed feathers found on the cage floor have desiccated follicles and will **not yield viable DNA** for PCR amplification.

| Feather Condition | Follicle Cells | DNA Viability | Test Reliability |
|:------------------|:---------------|:--------------|:-----------------|
| Freshly plucked | Present (visible bulb) | High | > 95% first-pass success |
| Shed (molted) | Absent or degraded | Very low | < 10% success rate |
| Stored > 4 weeks | Drying out | Declining | Increased re-test probability |
| Moldy or wet | Degraded | None | Sample rejected |

## Materials Needed

| Item | Purpose | Notes |
|:-----|:--------|:------|
| Clean paper envelope (per bird) | Sample container | **Never use plastic bags** — condensation causes mold |
| Disposable gloves | Prevent cross-contamination | Change gloves between birds |
| Sterile forceps (optional) | Handle small feathers | Useful for finches and budgies |
| Permanent marker | Labeling | Use waterproof ink |
| Breathable padded mailer | Shipping container | Protects feather follicles from crushing |

## Step-by-Step Collection Procedure

### Step 1: Restrain the Bird

Restrain the bird gently but securely. For small birds, use a towel or cupped hands. For larger birds (parrots, macaws), a second person may be needed to stabilize the head and body.

### Step 2: Identify the Collection Site

Select feathers from the **chest or flank** region. These feathers have the best follicle development and provide the highest DNA yield. Avoid wing and tail feathers — they are more painful to remove and the follicles may be damaged.

| Collection Site | Recommendation | Rationale |
|:----------------|:---------------|:-----------|
| Chest | Best choice | Large, well-developed follicles; minimal stress |
| Flank | Excellent alternative | Good follicle size; easy access |
| Wing coverts | Acceptable | Smaller follicles; more discomfort |
| Tail feathers | Not recommended | Painful; risk of follicle damage or bleeding |

### Step 3: Pluck the Feather

1. Grasp the feather close to the skin using thumb and forefinger
2. Pull firmly and quickly **in the direction of feather growth**
3. Inspect the follicle — a small **tissue bulb** should be visible at the feather base
4. If the bulb is absent or appears dry, the feather was shed — discard and collect another

### Step 4: Collect the Required Number of Feathers

| Bird Size | Examples | Feathers Required | Recommended Location |
|:----------|:---------|:-----------------:|:--------------------|
| Small | Budgie, finch, canary, parrotlet | 5–7 | Chest or flank |
| Medium | Conure, lovebird, cockatiel, pigeon | 3–5 | Chest or flank |
| Large | Macaw, cockatoo, African grey, Amazon | 2–3 | Chest |
| Extra-large | Emu, ostrich, swan | 1–2 | Chest or flank |

### Step 5: Place Feathers into Envelope

- Open the paper envelope and drop the feathers directly inside
- Do not touch the feather base with fingers — oils from skin can contaminate the sample
- Fold the envelope flap but do not seal until ready to ship (air circulation prevents mold)
- Label the envelope **immediately** with bird ID, collection date, species, and owner name

### Step 6: Verify Sample Quality

Before finishing the collection, confirm the following quality criteria:

- [ ] 3–7 feathers collected (per species guidance above)
- [ ] Follicle bulbs visible on all feathers
- [ ] No visible blood, fecal material, or dirt on feather shafts
- [ ] Feathers are dry and intact
- [ ] Envelope is labeled with bird ID and date
- [ ] Gloves changed between birds (if collecting from multiple birds)

## Critical Rules Table

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|:------|:---------|
| Pluck fresh feathers only — verify follicle bulb is present | Use shed feathers from the cage floor or perch |
| Label each bird's envelope immediately after collection | Mix multiple birds' feathers in one envelope |
| Use breathable paper envelopes | Seal feathers in plastic bags (condensation → mold) |
| Store at room temperature (15–25°C) in a dry, dark place | Refrigerate or freeze feathers |
| Ship within 2–4 weeks of collection | Expose to extreme heat, direct sunlight, or humidity |
| Collect from chest or flank | Pull wing or tail feathers unnecessarily |
| Change gloves between birds | Handle multiple birds without glove changes |

## Species-Specific Guidance

### Psittacines (Parrots, Macaws, Cockatoos)

Large psittacines have strong, well-developed chest feathers. Collect 2–3 feathers from the upper chest. For macaws, the feathers are thick — ensure the follicle bulb is intact after plucking.

### Passerines (Finches, Canaries, Sparrows)

These small birds have delicate skin and fine feathers. Use sterile forceps for better control. Collect 5–7 breast feathers. Watch for signs of stress and stop if the bird shows distress.

### Columbiformes (Pigeons, Doves)

Pigeons have loosely attached chest feathers that pluck easily. Collect 3–5 feathers. Pigeon feathers may have less visible follicle tissue — inspect closely and consider a blood or swab sample if follicle quality is poor.

### Galliformes (Chickens, Turkeys, Quail)

These birds have dense feather coverage. Collect 3–5 feathers from the breast or flank. Avoid blood feathers (newly forming feathers with a blood-filled shaft).

### Raptors (Hawks, Falcons, Owls)

Raptor feathers are tightly attached and require more force to pluck. Collect 2–3 chest feathers. For sensitive species, consider a blood or swab sample as an alternative.

### Waterfowl (Ducks, Geese, Swans)

Waterfowl have waterproof feathers with oil glands. Oil on the feather shaft may interfere with PCR — wipe the shaft gently with a clean tissue before placing in the envelope. Collect 2–3 flank feathers.

## Packaging for Shipment

| Step | Action | Key Point |
|:-----|:-------|:----------|
| 1 | Place labeled paper envelope(s) in a padded mailer | Do not add extra packaging material that could crush follicles |
| 2 | Include the SENO submission form | Ensure bird ID on envelope matches the form |
| 3 | Seal the padded mailer | Use tamper-evident tape if crossing borders |
| 4 | Attach the shipping label | Address to SENO's receiving department |
| 5 | Ship Monday–Wednesday | Avoid weekend holds at customs or courier facilities |

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|:--------|:-------------|:---------|
| No follicle bulb visible | Feather was shed or molt-stage | Re-pluck with firm, quick pull |
| Feather base is bloody | Cut a blood feather | Apply pressure; avoid bloody feathers |
| Feathers appear moldy | Stored in plastic or high humidity | Switch to paper envelopes; include desiccant |
| Low DNA yield | Too few feathers or old sample | Re-collect 5–7 fresh feathers |
| Bird shows stress during collection | Restraint or technique too rough | Stop, let bird rest, try again with gentler restraint |

## Why Paper Envelopes Are Mandatory

Feather follicles contain viable cells that **need to breathe**. Plastic bags trap residual moisture from the feather or environment, creating a microclimate that promotes mold and bacterial growth. Mold degrades DNA and renders the sample untestable. Paper envelopes allow air circulation while keeping the feathers contained.

| Container | Suitability | Reason |
|:----------|:------------|:-------|
| Paper envelope | ✅ Excellent | Breathable, lightweight, protects follicles |
| Cardboard box | ✅ Good with envelope inside | Adds structural protection for shipping |
| Plastic bag | ❌ Never use | Traps moisture, causes mold in 24–48 hours |
| Glass vial | ❌ Not recommended | Crushes follicles; no airflow |
| Aluminum foil | ❌ Avoid | Traps moisture; tears easily |
