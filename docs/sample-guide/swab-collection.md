# Swab Collection Protocol (Disease Testing)


Swab samples are the preferred sample type for avian disease testing at SENO. Oral and cloacal swabs are collected using sterile flocked swabs and immediately placed into nucleic acid stabilization buffer to preserve pathogen RNA and DNA for downstream molecular detection.

## When to Use Swab Samples

Swab-based disease testing is indicated in the following scenarios:

- **Respiratory disease investigation** — Oral/choanal swabs for detection of respiratory viruses and bacteria
- **Enteric disease investigation** — Cloacal swabs for gastrointestinal pathogen screening
- **Comprehensive health screening** — Combined oral + cloacal swabs for full pathogen panel
- **Quarantine or import screening** — Pre-movement health clearance testing
- **Outbreak investigation** — Rapid collection from multiple birds during disease outbreaks
- **Neonatal testing** — Safer than blood collection in very young chicks

## Swab Type Selection

| Swab Type | Collection Site | Target Pathogens | Best Use Case |
|:----------|:----------------|:-----------------|:--------------|
| Oral swab | Oropharynx, choanal slit | PiHV, PPMV-1, AIV, NDV, Mycoplasma, Avian Chlamydia | Respiratory disease screening |
| Cloacal swab | Cloaca | PiADV, Salmonella, E. coli, Avian Coronavirus, Enteric viruses | Enteric disease screening |
| Combined swab (both sites) | Oral + cloacal | All pathogens listed above | Comprehensive health certification |
| Ocular swab | Conjunctiva | Mycoplasma, Avian Chlamydia, AIV | Ocular infection cases |
| Tracheal swab | Trachea | AIV, NDV, IBV, Bordetella | Deep respiratory sampling |

## Materials Required

| Item | Quantity | Notes |
|:-----|:---------|:------|
| Sterile flocked swabs | 1–2 per bird | Flocked swabs collect 3–5× more cells than fiber swabs |
| Nucleic acid stabilization solution | 1 tube per swab | Provided by SENO; contains nuclease inactivators |
| Sterile gloves | 1 pair per bird | Change gloves between birds to prevent cross-contamination |
| Sample labels | 1 per tube | Waterproof, pre-printed or handwritten |
| Biohazard bag | 1 per shipment | Secondary containment for transport |
| Cold packs | As needed | For shipments > 48 hours from collection |
| Permanent marker | 1 | For writing on tube labels |

## Oral Swab Procedure

### Preparation
1. Put on a fresh pair of sterile gloves
2. Remove a sterile flocked swab from its packaging (avoid touching the tip)
3. Open the stabilization buffer tube and set the cap aside (keep clean)

### Collection Steps
1. **Gently restrain the bird** — towel restraint is recommended for parrots and other psittacines
2. **Open the beak** by applying gentle pressure on the commissures (corners of the mouth)
3. **Insert the swab into the oropharynx** — the cavity at the back of the mouth
4. **Rotate the swab** against the mucosal surface for **5–10 seconds** with gentle pressure
5. **Swab the choanal slit** — the narrow opening on the roof of the mouth (palate)
6. **Rotate again** for 3–5 seconds against the choanal mucosa
7. **Withdraw the swab carefully** — avoid touching the beak, tongue, or outside of the mouth
8. **Place the swab tip into the stabilization buffer tube**
9. **Break or cut the swab handle** at the break line (most flocked swabs have a pre-scored break point)
10. **Seal the tube tightly** — ensure the cap is fully closed to prevent leakage

| Step | Duration | Key Checkpoint |
|:-----|:---------|:---------------|
| Restraint | 10–15 seconds | Bird must be calm; excessive struggling reduces swab quality |
| Oral swabbing | 5–10 seconds | Adequate mucosal contact; visible moisture on swab |
| Choanal swabbing | 3–5 seconds | Swab contacts the slit on the palate |
| Buffer immersion | Immediate | Delay > 30 seconds may allow DNA/RNA degradation |

## Cloacal Swab Procedure

### Preparation
1. Use a fresh pair of sterile gloves (even if collecting oral and cloacal from the same bird)
2. Remove a new sterile flocked swab from packaging
3. Open a separate stabilization buffer tube

### Collection Steps
1. **Restrain the bird** with the vent (cloacal opening) exposed
2. **Moisten the swab tip** with sterile saline or stabilization buffer (optional — reduces friction)
3. **Insert the swab approximately 0.5–1 cm** into the cloaca (do not force)
4. **Rotate the swab gently** for 3–5 seconds — the swab tip should contact the cloacal mucosa
5. **Withdraw the swab** and inspect the tip for visible fecal material (a small amount is expected)
6. **Place the swab tip into the stabilization buffer tube**
7. **Break the swab handle** at the break line and seal the tube

| Parameter | Specification | Notes |
|:----------|:--------------|:------|
| Insertion depth | 0.5–1.0 cm | Do not exceed 1 cm in small birds |
| Rotation time | 3–5 seconds | Gentle rotation, not aggressive scraping |
| Fecal contamination | Expected | Small amount acceptable; heavy contamination may inhibit PCR |
| Buffer immersion | Immediate | Place into buffer within 30 seconds |

## Combined Oral + Cloacal Swab Protocol

For **comprehensive disease screening**, SENO recommends collecting both an oral and a cloacal swab from each bird. These can be placed into a **single stabilization buffer tube** (one swab per tube is still acceptable). When combining:

1. Collect the **oral swab first** (less contaminated)
2. Place the oral swab into the stabilization tube and break the handle
3. Collect the **cloacal swab** with a fresh swab
4. Add the cloacal swab to the **same tube**
5. Seal the tube and label as "Combined Oral + Cloacal"

## Stabilization Buffer Handling

The nucleic acid stabilization solution provided by SENO contains chemical agents that:

- Inactivate cellular and microbial nucleases (RNases and DNases)
- Precipitate nucleic acids in a protected state
- Prevent microbial growth during transport
- Maintain DNA and RNA integrity at ambient temperatures for up to 14 days

| Buffer Property | Detail |
|:----------------|:-------|
| Composition | Guanidine-based denaturing solution with chelating agents |
| pH | 6.5–7.5 (buffered) |
| Color | Colorless to pale yellow |
| Storage before use | Room temperature (15–25°C) |
| Shelf life (unopened) | 24 months from manufacture date |
| Volume per tube | 1.5 mL (sufficient for 1–2 swabs) |

**WARNING:** The stabilization buffer contains guanidine salts. Avoid contact with skin and eyes. If contact occurs, rinse with copious amounts of water. Do not ingest. Do not mix with bleach or chlorine-containing disinfectants — toxic gas may be produced.

## After Collection

### Labeling
Label each tube immediately after collection with the following information using a waterproof marker:

- Bird ID (or band number)
- Collection date (YYYY-MM-DD format preferred)
- Sample type (Oral / Cloacal / Combined)
- Collector initials

### Storage Before Shipment

| Shipping Timeline | Storage Condition | Notes |
|:-----------------|:------------------|:------|
| Within 24 hours | 2–8°C (refrigerator) | Cold pack recommended for summer shipments |
| Within 48 hours | 2–8°C (refrigerator) | Acceptable; buffer maintains integrity |
| 2–14 days | 2–8°C or −20°C | Freeze if > 48 hours for maximum RNA preservation |
| > 14 days | −20°C (freezer) | RNA integrity declines after 14 days at 4°C |

### Packaging for Shipment
1. Place sealed tubes upright in a tube rack or padded container
2. Place the rack inside a sealed biohazard bag
3. Place the biohazard bag inside an insulated box
4. Add cold packs (conditioned at 4°C) for ambient-temperature shipments
5. Add dry ice for frozen shipments — declare as UN3373 Biological Substance, Category B
6. Include the SENO submission form in a waterproof sleeve on top of the cold pack (not frozen)

## Quality Control Checkpoints

Before sealing the shipment, verify:

- [ ] Tubes are labeled with bird ID and date
- [ ] Tubes are sealed tightly (no buffer leakage)
- [ ] Swab tips fully submerged in stabilization buffer
- [ ] Gloves changed between each bird
- [ ] Cold packs are conditioned (not frozen) for refrigerated shipments
- [ ] Submission form is included and matches labels
- [ ] Biohazard bag is sealed

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|:--------|:-------------|:---------|
| Swab tip not immersed in buffer | Insufficient buffer volume | Tilt the tube so buffer covers the tip; or add a few drops of sterile PBS |
| Tube leaked during shipment | Cap not fully tightened | Double-bag tubes; use Parafilm around cap |
| Swab broke above the tube rim | Break line missed | Use scissors to trim the protruding handle |
| No visible material on swab | Insufficient mucosal contact | Re-swab with more rotation time (10–15 seconds) |
| Bird shows respiratory distress | Swab inserted too deeply | Remove immediately; ensure swab stays in oropharynx, not trachea |
| Cross-contamination suspected | Gloves not changed between birds | Request re-collection; discard contaminated samples |

## Pathogen Detection Guidelines by Swab Type

| Pathogen | Recommended Swab | Buffer Requirement | Stability Requirement |
|:---------|:-----------------|:-------------------|:----------------------|
| Psittacine Herpesvirus (PiHV) | Oral | Stabilization buffer | Ship within 7 days at 4°C |
| PPMV-1 (Paramyxovirus) | Oral/cloacal | Stabilization buffer | Ship frozen if > 48 hours |
| Avian Influenza (AIV) | Oral + cloacal | Stabilization buffer | Ship at 4°C; freeze if delay |
| Psittacine Adenovirus (PiADV) | Cloacal | Stabilization buffer | Room temp up to 14 days |
| Mycoplasma spp. | Oral/choanal | Stabilization buffer | 4°C preferred |
| Avian Chlamydia (Chlamydophila psittaci) | Oral/cloacal | Stabilization buffer | Ship at 4°C |
| Salmonella spp. | Cloacal | Standard transport medium | Room temp acceptable |
| Avian Coronavirus (IBV) | Oral/tracheal | Stabilization buffer | Freeze immediately for RNA preservation |

## Safety and Biosecurity

| Precaution | Rationale |
|:-----------|:----------|
| Wear gloves when handling swabs and buffer | Prevent chemical exposure and sample contamination |
| Change gloves between each bird | Prevent cross-contamination between samples |
| Dispose of used swabs and gloves in biohazard waste | Proper infection control |
| Work in a well-ventilated area | Buffer solution may irritate respiratory tract if aerosolized |
| Wash hands thoroughly after sample collection | Standard hygiene protocol |
| Clean work surfaces with 10% bleach solution | Inactivates residual nucleic acids and pathogens |
