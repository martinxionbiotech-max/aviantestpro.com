Columbiformes (Pigeons & Doves)¶

Overview¶

Pigeons represent a significant portion of SENO's testing volume, particularly racing pigeons (homing pigeons). SENO processes 600+ performance gene tests and 500+ virus PCR tests from pigeon samples monthly. The pigeon racing industry — particularly strong in Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, Taiwan, China, and the Middle East — drives demand for comprehensive genetic services that go beyond basic DNA sexing.

Background¶

Columbiformes (pigeons and doves) encompass over 300 species worldwide. The domestic pigeon (Columba livia domestica) was one of the first birds domesticated by humans (~5,000+ years ago), and selective breeding has produced an extraordinary diversity of breeds — from the high-velocity Racing Homer to the flamboyant Fantail and the vocal Trumpeter.

Pigeons differ from psittacines in several important respects for genetic testing:

  • CHD sexing is straightforward: ~25–30 bp fragment size difference means gel separation is reliable
  • Disease profile is distinct: PiCV, PPMV-1, and PiHV are the primary concerns, not PBFD
  • Performance genetics is a major field: Unlike parrots, pigeons have well-studied performance markers
  • Sample size is smaller: 300–500 g body weight makes blood collection safe and easy

Complete Testing Services for Pigeons¶

DNA Sexing¶

  • CHD-based PCR sexing using P2/P8 primer set

  • 99.5% accuracy with feather or blood samples

  • Reliable from day-old squab to adult
  • Sex ratio confirmation for balanced loft management

DNA Fingerprinting¶

  • Microsatellite-based individual genetic profile
  • 8–12 polymorphic microsatellite markers per bird
  • Applications: individual identification, theft prevention, registration verification

Parentage Verification¶

  • Genetic comparison of offspring with putative parents
  • Confirms or excludes parent-offspring relationships
  • Essential for pedigree validation and breeding record integrity

See Parentage Verification for methodology.

Frequently Asked Questions¶

Q: How early can I sex a racing pigeon? A: From day one. The CHD gene sequence is determined at fertilization — a squab's DNA carries the same sex information as an adult's. No need to wait for behavioral cues or sexual maturity (6–12 months).

Q: Can DNA fingerprinting replace bands/rings? A: No — rings are the primary identification for competition. However, fingerprinting serves as a secondary "genetic ring" that uniquely identifies the bird if a ring is lost, damaged, or swapped.

Q: How is the performance gene test useful if it doesn't predict racing winners? A: The test identifies genetic potential, not racing results. Used over multiple seasons and across a whole loft population, it helps breeders make data-informed pairing decisions — biasing towards combinations of favourable marker alleles that correlate with improved racing performance in population studies.

Q: What is the most common health issue in racing pigeons detected by PCR? A: PiCV (Pigeon Circovirus) is the most frequently detected pathogen in routine screening panels. PiCV causes immunosuppression that predisposes birds to secondary infections with other pathogens. It is particularly common in young birds and often underdiagnosed clinically.

Q: Do you test for Trichomonas? A: Trichomonas (canker) is a protozoal infection rather than a viral/bacterial pathogen, and is not included in SENO's standard 11-pathogen PCR panel. Trichomonas is typically diagnosed by microscopic examination of oral swabs by a veterinarian.
