    Blood Sample Collection Protocol¶

    Blood samples provide the highest DNA yield of all avian sample types accepted by SENO. This protocol covers three collection methods, safety procedures, and quality guidelines for submitting blood samples to our genetics laboratory.

    Indications for Blood Collection¶

    Blood samples are recommended when:

    • Feather samples are unavailable (e.g., featherless chicks, molting birds)
    • DNA fingerprinting or parentage verification is required (higher DNA yield improves resolution)
    • Certain disease testing requires blood (e.g., blood-borne pathogen screening, complete blood count correlation)
    • Sample age or storage history of alternative samples is uncertain
    • Maximum DNA quantity is needed for archival biobanking

    Important Considerations Before Collecting¶

    FactorConsideration
    Bird sizeMinimum blood volume scales with body weight — do not exceed 1% of body weight in 24 hours
    Stress levelMinimize handling time; stressed birds may experience complications
    AnticoagulantEDTA is preferred for DNA testing; heparin may inhibit PCR amplification
    Time of dayEarly morning collection reduces heat stress risk in warm climates
    Recent feedingFasted birds tolerate venipuncture better; avoid post-prandial lipemic samples

    Collection Method Comparison¶

    SENO accepts three blood collection methods. Choose the method that best suits your bird species, testing requirements, and shipping logistics.

    MethodDifficultyDNA YieldShipping EaseBest Use Case
    Venipuncture (EDTA tube)AdvancedVery highModerate (requires cold pack)Large birds, biobanking
    Blood spot (FTA card)ModerateHighExcellent (room temp)International shipping, routine testing
    Toenail clip (filter paper)EasyMediumExcellent (room temp)Small birds, neonates

    Method 1: Venipuncture (Recommended for Larger Birds)¶

    Equipment Required¶

    • Sterile syringe (1–3 mL) with 25–27 gauge needle
    • EDTA blood collection tube (purple top, 0.5–2 mL)
    • 70% ethanol or antiseptic wipe
    • Cotton ball or gauze
    • Disposable gloves
    • Bird-specific restraint towel
    • Permanent marker for labeling

    Venipuncture Sites¶

    SiteBird SizeAdvantagesDisadvantages
    Right jugular veinAll sizesEasy access, large vesselRequires trained restraint; left jugular is smaller
    Brachial (wing) veinMedium–largeVisible, easy accessHematoma risk; vessel rolls
    Medial metatarsal (leg) veinLarge birdsSafe for conscious birdsSmaller volume; slower collection

    Procedure¶

    1. Restrain the bird securely with a towel, exposing the chosen venipuncture site
    2. Locate the vein by visual inspection and gentle palpation
    3. Cleanse the site with 70% ethanol and allow to dry
    4. Insert the needle at a 15–30 degree angle, bevel up, with the needle pointing toward the heart
    5. Collect blood slowly — fill the syringe to the desired volume (0.1–0.5 mL for small birds; 0.5–1.0 mL for large birds)
    6. Remove the needle and apply firm pressure to the puncture site with sterile gauze for 30–60 seconds
    7. Transfer blood to the EDTA tube — gently invert 8–10 times to mix with anticoagulant
    8. Label the tube immediately with bird ID, collection date, and sample type
    ParameterSpecification
    Collection SiteJugular vein (right side), brachial vein, or medial metatarsal vein
    Volume0.1–0.5 mL (small birds); 0.5–1.0 mL (large birds)
    AnticoagulantEDTA (purple top tube) preferred for DNA testing
    Needle gauge25–27 gauge
    Maximum draw≤ 1% of body weight per 24-hour period

    Method 2: Blood Spot on FTA Card (Preferred for International Shipping)¶

    FTA cards contain proprietary chemicals that lyse cells, denature proteins, and stabilize nucleic acids at room temperature. This is the recommended method for clients shipping to SENO from abroad.

    Equipment Required¶

    • FTA card (Whatman FTA or equivalent)
    • Sterile lancet or needle
    • 70% ethanol wipe
    • Disposable gloves
    • Desiccant pack
    • Resealable plastic bag
    • Permanent marker

    Procedure¶

    1. Select a collection site — brachial vein or toenail clip (see Method 3)
    2. Collect fresh blood directly from the venipuncture site or free-flowing droplet
    3. Apply 1–2 drops to the FTA card's printed sample area (ensure the blood saturates the card, not pools on top)
    4. Air dry for 2–4 hours at room temperature (15–25°C) in a dust-free environment
    5. Do not stack wet cards — keep them separated until fully dry
    6. Place the dried card in a resealable bag with a desiccant pack
    7. Seal the bag and label with bird ID, date, collector name, and sample type
    8. No refrigeration required for shipping — the card stabilizes DNA at ambient temperature
    StepTime RequirementCritical Checkpoint
    Blood applicationImmediately after collectionEven saturation of the printed circle
    Air dry2–4 hoursCard must be dry to the touch; no visible moisture
    PackagingAfter dryingDesiccant pack must be included
    ShippingAny time after dryingFTA cards stable for years when kept dry

    Troubleshooting FTA Cards¶

    ProblemLikely CauseSolution
    Blood pools on surfaceCard surface is contaminated or waxedUse a fresh card; touch drop gently to the paper
    Insufficient blood volumeDroplet too smallCollect a larger drop; hold card directly at the site
    Card smells moldyDried before packagingEnsure 2–4 hour dry time; use fresh desiccant
    DNA yield is lowCard got wet in transitDouble-bag with extra desiccant for humid climates

    Method 3: Toenail Clip Blood Collection (Minimally Invasive)¶

    This method is suitable for very small birds (budgies, finches, canaries, chicks) where venipuncture is technically difficult or high-risk.

    Procedure¶

    1. Restrain the bird gently but securely
    2. Select a toenail — avoid the dewclaw if present
    3. Clean the nail with 70% ethanol
    4. Clip the tip of the nail (approximately 1–2 mm) using clean nail clippers
    5. Collect the blood droplet on a FTA card or sterile filter paper
    6. Apply gentle pressure with a cotton ball or styptic powder to stop bleeding
    7. Repeat on another nail if more volume is needed (maximum 2–3 nails)
    8. Monitor the bird for 5 minutes after the procedure to confirm hemostasis

    Limitations¶

    • Yields only 1–3 small drops (10–50 μL total)
    • Not suitable for EDTA tube collection (insufficient volume)
    • Higher risk of sample contamination from the foot surface

    Safety Precautions¶

    PrecautionExplanation
    Use sterile, single-use materialsPrevents cross-contamination and infection at the puncture site
    Apply pressure after collectionEnsures hemostasis; reduces hematoma risk
    Dispose of sharps immediatelyUse a puncture-resistant sharps container
    Label tubes and cards immediatelyPrevents sample misidentification
    Change gloves between birdsEliminates cross-contamination risk between different birds
    Never reuse needles or lancetsSingle-use only — reused instruments cause infection and sample contamination

    MethodStorage Before ShipmentMaximum Hold TimeShipping Condition
    EDTA whole blood2–8°C (refrigerate)7 daysCold pack (2–8°C)
    FTA card (dried)Room temperature (15–25°C)YearsRoom temperature
    Filter paper spotRoom temperature (15–25°C)30 daysRoom temperature

    Packing for Shipment¶

    • EDTA tubes: Wrap in bubble wrap, place in a sealed plastic bag with absorbent material, ship with a cold pack in an insulated container
    • FTA cards: Place in a resealable bag with desiccant, insert into a padded envelope
    • Filter paper spots: Same as FTA cards; protect from moisture with desiccant

    When Not to Use Blood Samples¶

    Blood collection may be contraindicated in the following situations:

    • Severely dehydrated or anemic birds (increased complication risk)
    • Birds with clotting disorders or on anticoagulant therapy
    • Extremely small neonates (< 10 g body weight) — use swab or feather instead
    • Birds that cannot be safely restrained
    • STAT testing where minimal handling time is critical — consider swab collection
